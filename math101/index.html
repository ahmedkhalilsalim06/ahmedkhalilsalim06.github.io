Limits, continuity, derivatives and their applications, integration and the transcendental functions. The backbone is Okan Tekman’s archive: every Fall midterm and final question from 1998 to 2025, one web page per question, still live on his server. Metin Gürses, Alex Degtyarev, Aydan Pamir and G. Özkan added full semesters of their own. Catalog entry.

Everything below was posted publicly by the course’s instructors, or shared by students on GitHub. LIVE links go to the file on the Bilkent server. ARCHIVE links open the copy the Internet Archive took while the page was public. REPO links are files a student shared in a public GitHub repository, not checked against the official version. Grade lists, attendance sheets, rosters and exam-room lists were left out because they name students.

Use these for practice, not submission. Old solutions show how a problem was graded in that year. Handing them in breaks Bilkent’s academic-integrity rules.

Current material lives on Moodle. For this semester’s slides and exams, use Moodle (from campus or the university VPN). STARS holds the official syllabus for every section since 2010.

What was searched

  • Mathematics faculty list and every ~user folder it links on fen.bilkent.edu.tr, grepped for 101 and calculus
  • Filename probing: Tekman’s semester folders f98–f25 (every exam and question number), Gürses’s and Degtyarev’s exam naming
  • Wayback CDX: fen.bilkent.edu.tr filtered for 101, then full listings of each folder it named
  • Left out: final-exam room lists (Gürses 2011, Degtyarev 2011), which name students
  • Student repos (25 Sep 2026): about 1,430 public GitHub repositories whose name, description or topics mention Bilkent were collected, and the full file lists of about 580 of them (every coursework repo and monorepo among them) were searched, along with code-name searches for this course. One useful find: a student’s complete Fall 2016 lecture notes (listed under notes). A second copy of Tekman’s compiled question bank sits in MAKman1/Bilkent-CS-Curriculum and is not listed twice.
